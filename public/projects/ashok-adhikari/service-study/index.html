Pokhara municipal-code gate — do not choose either sketch yet

Options A and B are NOT ADMISSIBLE AS DRAWN under the earlier published municipal setback note. Both tank/pit candidates sit within the modeled 3,126 mm front setback. The published first-amended Pokhara building standard includes a prohibition on tanks/pits inside setbacks and a neighbour-boundary separation rule subject to the specified consent provision.

Earlier municipal text §10.15 also specifies septic length:width ratio 2–4 and a ventilation extension 2 m above the building top. This model has an outer tank rectangle ratio 2,300/1,300 = 1.77 and documents only a 0.9 m roof vent extension. The engineered internal tank dimensions, actual roof levels and ventilation arrangements must be checked rather than assumed to pass.

Earlier §10.15.1 includes 3 m septic-to-drinking-water-tank separation; §10.14.1 includes 16 m between a drinking-water well and listed waste-disposal pits. No source-confirmed well, water tank or neighbour locations are available.

Current edition question: Pokhara posted a third amendment (2083) on 14 October 2026. Its substantive effect on these clauses has not yet been verified. Do not declare the older provisions unchanged or issue construction documents without checking the current municipal text. Source: official Pokhara acts, laws and directives.

Conditional envelope result: excluding the front setback and assuming a 1,000 mm neighbour-border margin, the nominal 1,817 mm left, 1,524 mm rear and 127 mm right strips cannot accept either currently modeled external vessel envelope without revisiting building/site design. This is not a determination that no legal alternative exists.

Can septic and soak pit go beneath the building footprint?

Being inside the plinth footprint may remove an object from the nominal open-setback strip, but does not create automatic legal or engineering approval. A sealed septic vessel and an infiltration soak pit are not interchangeable.

Required evidence before choosing a location

  1. Surveyed boundary coordinates, road edge/right of way, site levels and confirmed owner parcel.
  2. Foundation layout: actual footing/grade beam/founding levels, basement or excavations (if any), safe distances and temporary excavation support.
  3. Measured site percolation and seasonal high-groundwater table; drinking-water well/spring locations on and near the property.
  4. Exterior WC status: permitted location, wall geometry, doorway, plumbing and maintenance access.
  5. Existing sanitation collection layout: drains, cleanouts, gravity slope/inverts, venting and inspection access.
  6. Vehicle/entry model: correct automobile envelope, parking, actual gate/driveway, turning path and load protection.
  7. Municipal permission for the selected treatment and discharge strategy; sewer network availability and approval.

Decision gate: responsible architect, structural engineer and sanitary/MEP engineer review the selected candidate against original records. No actual tank relocation, model revision, dimensioned construction detail or v3.7 edition is authorized by this feasibility screen.
